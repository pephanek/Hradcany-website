News

What has been added to the site — new articles, additions to the records and updates to the statistics. The news log itself is kept in Czech.

  • 6. 9. 2026 15h — The records kept growing: the statistics now rest on 22,029 attributed stamps, 6,818 of them dated. The biggest gain was in perforation D (11½ line, plates 1–2) — up from 750 to 855 pieces. Kařez has been added to the list of post offices documented on perforation E, which now numbers 161 offices and is also on the cancellation map.
  • 6. 9. 2026 15h — Processing of the stock sheets of strips and blocks is complete: the statistics now rest on 21,922 attributed stamps, 6,790 of them dated. The cancellation map gains Varnsdorf and Cheb on plate III, Karlovy Vary and Heřmanův Městec on plate IV, and Chyše, Zábřeh and Ústí nad Orlicí in the layer of perforation A on plates V–VI. The list of post offices documented on perforation B has been reconciled with the map — it now has 60 offices, and every one of them (except the still unlocated Schenkenhahn) is on the map. Double and multiple print gains a fourth piece: the second impression is shifted up and to the right and rotated, so that the inscription ČESKO-SLOVENSKÁ runs obliquely through the middle of the stamp. The Colours and shades page gains Marek Vrba’s practical key (Filatelie 11/2013): how the five catalogue shades behave under the UV lamp and how to recognise chemically recoloured forgeries of the brownish red 7a.
  • 5. 9. 2026 15h — The Postal use of the 15h page now covers the last day of the Hradčany's validity, 30 April 1921: four pieces from that day span all three main perforations of the later plates — field 64/IV (perforation A), fields 99/IV and 46/IV (perforation B), and field 16/V with a machine cancel (perforation C). A new article, Cancellations dated after the end of validity, documents a block of four 15h stamps from plate 6 (fields 48/49/58/59) with a cancellation dated 13 June 1921 — over a month after validity ended, most likely due to a misset date wheel.
  • 4. 9. 2026 15h — The Postal use examples page now gives a full attribution for every cover: the sheet position and printing plate of each 15 h, the perforation, the accompanying stamps, the place and date of posting, the tariff period and the type of franking. Two items have been added that already had articles of their own — a letter bearing fields 9 and 19 of the rarest plate VII (Humpolec, 3 August 1919) and a postally used imperforate 15h from plate VI (Košice 2, 13 July 1920). Seventeen covers in all.
  • 4. 9. 2026 15h — The cancellation map gains Rynoltice and Mikulovice on plate III and the travelling post office 113 Broumov–Choceň; the position of Prácheň has been corrected. A copy from Košice 2 (field 29/V, 26 June 1920) has been added to the postally used imperforate stamps of plates 5 and 6, which now number thirteen. The statistics have been updated to 6 527 dated cancellations. Plate VII gains Horní Benešov (19 July 1919) and a second copy from Karviná (7 October 1919).
  • 4. 9. 2026 15h — The pages on printing have been expanded. Proofs gains a section of colour trials: the same design pulled in black, brown-black, blue and several shades of red, one on pinkish paper. Being imperforate and uncancelled, they show the engraving down to the last stroke and can be set against a production stamp from the same sheet position. Production flaws gains two chapters: double and multiple printing, where the sheet went through the press twice and every line has a twin — sometimes offset by tenths of a millimetre, sometimes shifted far and rotated — and printing and offset on the gum, where two seemingly identical faults are told apart by a mirror test: a direct impression on the gum reads the right way round and proves the paper was gummed before printing, whereas an offset from the neighbouring sheet is mirrored and paler.
  • 1. 9. 2026 New article – a pair of 15h stamps from the seventh printing plate on one letter (Humpolec, 3 August 1919, fields 9 and 19 of plate VII). Plate VII is the poorest documented of all seven plates — the site's records include only 122 attributed copies covering 70 fields — making this find of two different fields on a single letter one of the most significant additions to the plate's records.
  • 1. 9. 2026 Issue — A new Postal use page on the main site now covers the general postal use of the Hradčany issue and an overview of the tariffs and postal rates in force in 1918–1921 (previously part of the 15h site). On the 15h site, Postal use of the 15h now also covers the issue date and postal role of the 15 h value itself; the note restricting the data to printing plates 1 and 2 has moved directly into the railway-post paragraph. The section also gained its first example: the article Sokol regional rally in Znojmo, 24 Aug 1919 on the earliest documented use of a Czechoslovak commemorative postmark on the Hradčany issue — with the cover, a second recorded copy and a look at the postmark itself.
  • 31. 8. 2026 15h — New section Thin print with ink spots in Production flaws: ink that is too thin and unevenly spread gives a weak, faded impression, yet in the very same places of the design it sometimes collects into small, solidly red spots — with seven recorded copies and an analysis of why it is neither a blank spot, a wandering ring, a smear, nor a světlina.
  • 30. 8. 2026 15h — The Production flaws page now offers a complete overview of printing and paper flaws: Partly doubled print (5 items), Wandering rings (14 items), Smears (19 items) and Blank spots (4 items) under Printing flaws; Creased paper (24 items) and Splinters in the paper (3 items) under Paper flaws. Under Damage to the printing plate, both articles – the cracked plate at 11/II and the damaged frame at 60/VI – are now shown directly on the page instead of just linked. The index at the top now links straight to each subcategory, and the fixed cancel map now correctly shows places under individual plate filters too, not only under “multiple groups”.
  • 30. 8. 2026 15h — New acquisition: a piece with partially doubled printing (perforation A, plate 1 or 2), with a dated cancellation of 1 June 1920; the post office is not legible — this is the first documented dated example of this perforation on plates 1–2. Now featured on the Production flaws page and in the statistics (6,477 dated cancellations).
  • 30. 8. 2026 15h — New acquisition: an imperforate vertical pair (15h vermilion) from plate V, fields 6+16, cancelled Košice 4, 1 September 1920 – postally used imperforate plate 5 is scarce (now 10 dated pieces).
  • 29. 8. 2026 15h — New addition to the records: a piece with perforation E (plate II) with a dated cancellation of 10 March 1920; the post office is not legible. The statistics have been updated (6,474 dated cancellations).
  • 26. 8. 2026 15h — New section Perforation shift – how the shift of the perforating grid against the design arises, what degrees it takes and why it is not a plate flaw; with a gallery of 28 items (29 stamps), from barely perceptibly unequal margins to pieces where the perforation runs through the design and a strip of the neighbouring stamp is left at the edge.
  • 24. 8. 2026 15h — New additions to the records: a horizontal pair with perforation H (Turnov, 15 March 1920) – the earliest recorded use of perforation H so far – two pieces with perforation E, Hoštka/Gastorf (31 December 1919, a post office new to the E records) and Česká Lípa/Böhm. Leipa (September 1919), and eight imperforate stamps from plates V and VI cancelled Košice, Plešivec, Lučenec, Brezovka and Malý Lipník. The statistics (6,416 dated cancels) and the cancel map have been updated.
  • 26. 7. 2026 15h — New article – a postally used imperforate 15h from the sixth plate (Košice 2, 13 July 1920, field 34/VI). The 15h cancel map now also covers legible cancels from plates 3 and 4.
