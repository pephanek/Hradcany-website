The matrix clearly shows the scarcity of perforation A on plates V–VI (501 pieces, i.e. ≈5 % of the material of these plates — against ≈47 % each for B and C), the scarcity of C on plates I–II (26 pieces), and the exclusivity of line perforations D, E and F to the early plates I–II.

The sequence of earliest dates nicely documents the production chronology: imperforate sheets and line perforations D → E → F on plates 1–2 (summer–autumn 1919), then C and B, and from spring 1920 plates 3–6 and comb perforation A.

Perforation B: share of plates I and II

A separate attribution of 413 perforation-B pieces from plates I–II: plate I 105 pieces (25.4 %), plate II 308 pieces (74.6 %) — plate II thus clearly dominates in perforation B.

Perforation shares in collections compared

The differences from the Monograph (e.g. for perforation B) suggest that the historical rarity data for some perforations deserve revision.
